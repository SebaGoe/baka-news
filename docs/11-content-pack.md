# 11 · Content Pack (writing the ridiculous news)

Content lives in `data/content/articles.json`. 20 starter stories ship across 7 categories
and ~14 languages. Your job: keep the voice, add more, keep all three translations filled.

## The voice
- **Premise-absurd, delivered deadpan.** Write it like a real wire report about an
  impossible thing. The comedy is the straight face. (See the German broken-traffic-light or
  the litigious Italian pineapple.)
- **Three beats per story** usually: the absurd fact → a deadpan quote/detail → a dumb
  escalation or button. Briefs can be two.
- **Cute, kind, clean.** No real named people/brands as targets. No stereotypes — the butt
  of the joke is the surreal premise, never a nationality or group. A country is just the
  *setting* for whimsy.

## Writing checklist per article
1. Pick a `country` + `flag` + original `lang`.
2. Assign `size` (mix them per category) and ≥1 `categories`.
3. Write `headline`, `dek`, and 2–3 `body` paragraphs **in the original language**.
4. Translate faithfully-but-playfully into `en` and `ja` (keep the joke, not the literal
   words). If unsure of the original language, write it in English and set `lang: "en"`.
5. Make the **first body paragraph** strong — it's the card lead and gets a drop cap.
6. Give a silly byline in-language ("Sorry McApologyface", "田中・ぶんきち記者").
7. Unique `id` slug. Recent-ish `date` (controls ordering).

## Translation tips (EN ⇄ JA)
- Match register: deadpan-official in all three languages.
- Japanese: use natural news-desk phrasing; keep the punchline intact. Don't romanize.
- Don't machine-literal a pun — re-land it. If a pun won't translate, swap for an equivalent
  bit of nonsense in that language.

## Coverage goals
- Every category has at least one `lead`/`feature` and several `standard`/`brief`.
- Spread countries/languages; avoid repeating the same country back-to-back on the front.
- Target 40–60 articles total for a lively front page (20 shipped — add ~30 more).

## Seed prompts (turn these into full trilingual entries)
- A country declares Monday optional; productivity unchanged.
- Penguin passes the bar exam, immediately objects to everything.
- Museum exhibits an empty frame titled "Wi-Fi (2026)", crowds gather.
- Village elects a very confident goose as weather forecaster; accuracy: vibes.
- Scientists confirm the floor is, in fact, sometimes lava (Tuesdays).
- National orchestra replaced by one very committed kazoo.
- Man returns library book 40 years late, fined one (1) firm handshake.
- Toaster achieves enlightenment, still burns the toast.

## The 30-year backlog (`data/content/articles-backlog.json`)
Ships with **30 stories, one per calendar year 1996–2025**, from ~20 countries and ~20
languages — a talking Japanese vending machine (1998), a French croissant ruled "too flaky
to regulate" (2000), a Korean robot-vacuum union (2006), a man suing gravity (2016), Swiss
trains so punctual they arrive before departing (2025), and more. Each is fully trilingual
(native / EN / JA) and tagged with `size`/`categories` so it slots straight into the front
page, search, and RSS.

It was generated by `tools/build_backlog.py` — authoring the data as Python dicts keeps the
multilingual JSON valid. To extend or regenerate: edit that script and run
`python3 tools/build_backlog.py`, or just hand-write another `articles-*.json` file (see the
import note in doc 02). The app auto-merges all `articles*.json` files by `id`.
